# Fire, smoke and closures

**This is the go/no-go page for this trip.** Mid-September is the tail of fire season in the
Washington Cascades. On a route made almost entirely of Forest Service roads inside one national
forest, a single incident can close a road, a campground, or an entire area with little notice.
Terrain is not the risk on this trip. Fire, smoke and closures are.

Check these the week before departure, the morning you leave, and every morning you have signal
(realistically Packwood and Randle, plus Carson at the start).

---

## Active fire: High Lava

The **High Lava Fire** is burning approximately 10–11 miles northwest of Stabler, WA, in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest between Mt. St. Helens and Mt. Rainier. Lightning ignited it on July 23, 2026. As of early September it is smoldering with no perimeter growth; recent rain has aided containment.

**Status as of September 7, 2026:** ~2,899 acres · 63% contained · Stage 2 fire restrictions in effect across all of GPNF

**Impact on this itinerary:** The closure covers Wind River, Trapper Creek, Canyon Creek, Siouxon Creek, and the Soda Peaks and West Crater trailheads — the far western fringe of GPNF, well away from the main route corridor. FS 23, FS 25, Elk Pass, Takhlakh, Walupt, and High Rock are all outside the closure boundary. **No planned stops on this itinerary are inside the closed area.** The fire is the direct cause of the Stage 2 restrictions that ban all campfires, including at dispersed sites.

## What a closure actually does to this route

The loop has few alternates. Most of it is a single road corridor, so a closure usually means
skipping a segment rather than routing around it.

| If this closes | Effect | Workaround |
|---|---|---|
| FS 23 over Babyshoe Pass (mi 60–92) | Cuts Day 1 from Indian Heaven to Takhlakh | Back out to Trout Lake and approach Takhlakh from the east via FS 88 / FS 23 north |
| Walupt Lake Rd (mi 133) | Loses the Day 2 camp | Camp Chambers Lake or Adams Fork instead |
| High Rock spur (mi 177) | Loses the best viewpoint | Skip it; the day gets 2 hours shorter |
| FS 25 / Elk Pass (mi 250) | Breaks the Day 4 return | Exit north to US 12 at Randle and run the highway to Carson, abandoning miles 250–325 |
| Wind River Rd (mi 0–20) | Blocks the start | Enter the loop from Trout Lake and run it backwards |
| Whole-forest closure | Trip is off | Decide by the Friday before and pick a different area |

If the forest goes into an area closure or Industrial Fire Precaution Level 4, assume the trip is
cancelled rather than trying to salvage a partial route.

## Fire restriction levels and what they mean for camp

Gifford Pinchot typically runs a tiered restriction scheme in late summer. Read the current order
rather than assuming, but in general:

- **No restrictions** — campfires allowed in fire rings at developed campgrounds and at existing
  dispersed sites.
- **Stage 1** — campfires only inside metal fire rings at designated developed campgrounds. This
  is the most likely state for early September, and it means the **paid backups** on this
  itinerary can still have a fire but the **primary dispersed nights cannot**.
- **Stage 2** — no campfires anywhere, including developed campgrounds. Gas stoves with a shutoff
  valve are usually still permitted.
- **Area or forest closure** — no entry.

**Bring a gas stove regardless.** Do not plan any meal that depends on a campfire. Primary
nights are free dispersed / primitive sites. Under Stage 1 those usually cannot have a
campfire at all; only the paid developed backups keep a legal ring. A Stage 2 order removes
fires everywhere.

Also worth carrying: a shovel and a fire extinguisher or a few gallons of water per vehicle. Some
forest orders require them for anyone operating off pavement during fire season.

## Smoke

Smoke is more likely to affect this trip than flame. September smoke in the south Cascades often
drifts in from fires in Oregon, the eastern Cascades, or British Columbia rather than from
anything nearby.

What it costs you: the entire point of High Rock Lookout, Burley Mountain, Council Bluff, Steamboat
Mountain and Takhlakh Lake is the view. Heavy smoke turns all of them into a grey wall. It is not
dangerous at moderate levels, but it can make the scenic core of the route pointless.

Judgement calls:

- **AQI under 100** — go, expect hazy photos.
- **AQI 100 to 150** — go, but consider dropping the lookout hikes and spending the time at the
  lakes instead. Anyone with asthma should be cautious.
- **AQI over 150** — seriously reconsider. Views are gone and prolonged exertion outdoors is
  genuinely unhealthy.

Check [AirNow](https://fire.airnow.gov/) for the forecast, not just the current reading. Smoke can
clear or arrive in a day with a wind shift.

## Weather that closes roads

Fire is not the only closure risk in September.

- **Early snow** at elevation. Babyshoe Pass is around 4,350 ft and the High Rock spur climbs
  higher. An early-season storm can make gravel switchbacks genuinely bad. Check
  [WSDOT mountain passes](https://wsdot.com/travel/real-time/mountainpasses) and the
  [White Pass corridor](https://wsdot.com/travel/real-time/mountainpasses/white).
- **Heavy rain** on the west side. It does not usually close roads, but it makes clay and pumice
  surfaces slick and can wash out the rough spurs.
- **Wind** in timber. Blowdown across a Forest Service road is common and the forest may not clear
  a minor road quickly. Carry a saw.

See the [weather page](weather.html) for the dual NWS and Open-Meteo forecast for each camp.

## Before you leave the pavement each morning

A short discipline that costs five minutes:

1. Refresh the forest alerts page while you still have signal at Carson, Packwood or Randle.
2. Note the current fire restriction stage and say it out loud to the group.
3. Confirm the day's camp is still open, and that its access road is not on a closure list.
4. Check the smoke forecast if views are the reason you are going somewhere.
5. Tell the satellite check-in contact where the group intends to sleep.

Once you leave Randle on Day 4 you have roughly 108 miles with no coverage and no fuel. Anything
you did not check at Randle, you are not going to know.
