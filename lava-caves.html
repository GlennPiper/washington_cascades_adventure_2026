Falls Creek Lava Caves

Saturday Sep 12, route mile 298. A real lava tube on Gifford Pinchot National Forest, formed by the Big Lava Bed flow about 8,200 years ago. It is the wild version of a lava tube: no lights, no stairs, no staff, no cell.

This is not Ape Cave. Ape Cave, on Mount St. Helens, is a developed interpretive site that needs a timed Recreation.gov ticket. These caves do not.

The itinerary budgets 90 minutes, and the stop starts checked in the scheduler so Saturday's arrival at Panther Creek already includes it. Uncheck to skip.

Permits and fees

No cave-entry permit. No paid admission. No timed ticket.
Cave-entry permit None
Timed ticket / admission None
Wilderness permit No. The cave is not inside Indian Heaven Wilderness
Parking at the cave Informal pullout on FR 6701 / spur 601. Not a developed fee lot
Northwest Forest Pass Bring one anyway. Other Saturday trailheads, and Falls Creek Falls (a different stop), are $5 day-use sites

You do not need a pass just to drive into Gifford Pinchot. Fees are site-by-site. Some blogs say otherwise; they are wrong.

Confirm nothing has closed the week of the trip:

  • Gifford Pinchot NF alerts — there is no forest-wide cave closure as of late August 2026. The only cave forest order on that list is Ape Cave's.
  • Mt Adams Ranger District (Trout Lake) — (509) 395-3402. Mon / Wed / Fri 9–4. Ask: "Is Falls Creek Cave open, any bat closure, any issue on FR 6701?"

September 12 is well outside typical bat hibernation (roughly November–April), so a seasonal hibernaculum closure is unlikely. Still ask.

What it actually is

The main cave is about 1.2 miles of unmaintained lava tube, one of the longer ones in Washington that the Forest Service does not develop. Three collapse pits (breakdowns) are visible from the surface. You enter through the second pit, dropping in over piles of jagged basalt.

Inside: pitch dark, about 40°F year-round, uneven floor, side passages, slippery clay, and rockfall. The GPX pin is at 45.94473, −121.86807, about 0.6 miles off the driving track. Parking is a roadside pullout about 75 yards from the pits; there are no signs.

Six vehicles at a small spur: do not block FR 6701.

Gear, or do not go in

This is self-rescue caving. The Forest Service does not patrol it.

  • One headlamp per person, plus a backup light and spare batteries. A phone flashlight is not enough, and one shared light for the group is not enough.
  • Helmet, or at minimum a beanie. The ceiling is basalt.
  • Gloves and sturdy boots. The rock is sharp.
  • Warm layer. Forty degrees underground after a sunny forest-road day is a surprise.
  • No dogs. They spread white-nose fungus in fur and paws, and the rock shreds pads.

Do not treat this as a walk. If anyone in the group is uneasy scrambling on loose, sharp rock in the dark, skip the interior and look down into the collapse pits from the surface. That is still worth the stop.

White-nose syndrome

This is the rule that actually matters. White-nose syndrome is a fungal disease that has killed millions of bats. It is in Washington (first case 2016, now many counties). It does not affect people. People spread it on boots and kit.

Gifford Pinchot's policy is to keep caves open by decontaminating, not shut them. Practical version for this trip:

  • Do not wear boots or kit that has been in another cave unless it has been decontaminated.
  • Brush mud off on site. Then hot-wash clothing and boots at 131°F / 55°C, or follow the national WNS protocol.
  • A normal laundry cycle does not count as decontamination.
  • If you see bats, leave them alone. Do not handle live or dead bats. Report sick or dead ones to WDFW.

How to find it

From the Saturday driving corridor, near route mile 298:

  1. FR 6701 to the junction with spur 601 (a pullout by a gravel pit).
  2. Park without blocking the road. Trailhead roughly 45.94391, −121.86942.
  3. Short walk to three collapse pits. Enter at the second.

The Falls Creek Falls trailhead (FR 3062 / spur 057) is a different parking lot, with a vault toilet and a $5 day-use fee. That is the waterfall, not the cave.
